The Whiplash Index

Where the economy stands vs the story being told about it · updated September 14, 2026

What it says right now: The picture got more mixed, and the dot moved toward the middle. Freight, the measure of actual goods moving around the country, grew compared with a year ago for the first time in a long while. But small businesses are still cutting workers and hours, and inflation is heating back up because of fuel prices. The story told by markets and official numbers is still running hotter than the economy underneath, just by a little less than before.

What just changed: Shipping volumes rose instead of falling, a real point in the economy’s favor. Gas prices pushed inflation up to 3.4%, and the cost of long-term government borrowing climbed to nearly 5%, the highest in years, even though the government has started buying its own bonds to hold rates down. Markets now overwhelmingly expect the Fed to raise rates this week.

What we are waiting on: The Federal Reserve decides on rates this week, and what the Fed does next is the big card to turn over. Japan’s central bank is expected to raise its own rates too, which matters for money borrowed cheaply there and invested everywhere else. And we are waiting to see whether the rebound in shipping lasts a second month.

How it is measured

The left-right position comes only from private data: freight volumes, restaurant traffic, and private labor readings, mapped so the middle means a flat economy. The up-down position is the “extension” reading, like an RSI for the economic story: how far the official numbers AND market prices are running from what the private data shows. It blends the gap between official and independent inflation measures, whether official and private jobs data point the same direction, and whether stocks and credit are priced for the economy the private data describes. The path shows quarterly averages over a 3-year window. Data referenced: Cass Freight Index, Truflation, BLS CPI, ICE BofA high-yield spreads (via FRED), S&P 500.
